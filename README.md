## Overview

The Roperite is a creature from the corpus of fearsome critters — humorous tall-tale beasts told in North American logging camps in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. It was placed in the published canon by William T. Cox in his 1910 book 'Fearsome Creatures of the Lumberwoods, With a Few Desert and Mountain Beasts'. According to Cox, the Roperite is about the size of an undernourished pony and lived in herds in the Sierra foothills of California, especially among the Digger pine country, but had not been reported in herds for many years by 1910. Its defining feature is a long, flexible, rope-like beak that it uses to lasso prey at speed; victims are then dragged through chaparral until dead. Cox's text relays a Digger-Indian (a colonial-era exonym for Indigenous Californian peoples) tradition that Roperites are the spirits of early Spanish ranchers, accounting for the lariat behaviour. The Roperite has no purported real-world basis: it is a literary preservation of a logging-camp joke.
